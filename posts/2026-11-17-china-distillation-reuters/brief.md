## Facts (all from article 0)
Source: AI타임스 (AI Times Korea), "중국, 미국 AI '증류' 산업화…자동 수집에 전문 인력까지 투입", 2026-11-17, https://www.aitimes.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=217497 (citing Reuters)
1. Reuters analysed more than 300 Chinese papers, patents, company filings, job postings and government documents and reported that distillation (using US model outputs to train models) has moved beyond academic experiments into industrial use.
2. Chinese researchers obtain answers, code and solutions to complex problems from US models to train their own. Companies have built software that automates the collection and use specialists to process the training material.
3. According to three people, at a July meeting in Chengdu US Commerce Department officials raised the collection of outputs from major US AI models with Chinese regulators and warned some of it could amount to IP infringement.
4. A US official said Chinese authorities described distillation as a normal AI development technique but said they could act against IP infringement, and made no specific commitment.
5. Some Chinese developers recently asked the industry regulator for criteria separating ordinary use of foreign models from systematic collection of outputs for training.
6. The Chinese government encourages distillation for industrial AI: a January policy from eight ministries, "AI+Manufacturing", includes support for training, fine-tuning and distillation. The document does not tell companies to distil US models.
7. Distillation is not illegal in itself and is used in other countries. OpenAI and Anthropic restrict by their terms the use of their outputs to build competing models.
