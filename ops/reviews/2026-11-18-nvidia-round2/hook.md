Chosen hook: "NVIDIA GUIDES **$130B**, NO CHINA"

**Score: 7/10. Verdict: PASS.** No better option; keep option 0.

## Scores by criterion
- Clear in under 2 seconds: 7. Company, number and "no China" read fast on the cover. "Guides" is finance jargon and some global readers will not parse it at once.
- Curiosity: 7. "Why no China?" pulls the reader to slide 3 and the H200 exit.
- Concrete number or name: 9. Nvidia plus $130B. The yellow $130B stands out.
- Promise the slides keep: 8. Slide 3 ("No China data center compute in outlook") gives $130.0B +/-3% and says Nvidia is "not assuming any" China Data Center compute revenue. The cover's "NO CHINA" is a compressed form of that scope. "Guides" limits it to the outlook, and the slides state the exact scope. Slide 2 (the first slide after the cover) shows the $115.8B quarter, so the $130B payoff comes one swipe later, on slide 3. This is acceptable.
- Reader stake: 3. Nothing says what changes for an ordinary reader. The articles state no such effect, so this is not held against the hook.

## Compared with the other options
- Option 1 ("NVIDIA'S H200 EXITS CHINA"): accurate, but "H200" is unexplained on the cover and it has no money number. It is less clear and has less pull.
- Option 2 ("NVIDIA'S $115.8B CHINA LEFT OUT"): "left out" is vague about what was left out. It pairs the quarter's revenue with China in a way that suggests China was left out of that revenue, which the slides do not say.
- Option 3 ("NVIDIA'S $44B CHINA RULE RISK"): the $44B is a Jefferies forecast (range $44B to $62B) and is shown without attribution. It is the riskiest for promise-keeping.
- The chosen hook is the best balance of clarity, number and honest scope.
